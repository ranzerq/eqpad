Guide

1. The display and caret

Type on your keyboard or click the keypad. Click anywhere in the equation to move the caret. Drag, or hold Shift and press the arrow keys, to select; Ctrl/Cmd+A selects everything. Typing, DEL or Backspace replaces or deletes a selection. ← → move through the equation and in and out of fractions, roots and powers. ↑ ↓ jump between the top and bottom of a fraction. Backspace on an empty fraction, root or power removes it; on a filled one it first steps into it.

2. Fractions (a⁄b or /)

With a selection, the selection becomes the numerator, so you can write 53 + 91, select it and press a⁄b. Without a selection the key takes what is directly left of the caret: a run of digits (decimal point included), a single variable, Greek letter, function or root, or a whole bracketed group (its outer brackets are dropped), plus any powers or subscripts attached to it. The caret then jumps to the denominator. Press / again inside a numerator or denominator for nested fractions.

Mixed fractions. The mixed-fraction key (𝑎𝑏⁄𝑐, next to ≠) writes a whole number followed by a fraction, like 2¾. Type the whole number first (or select something), press the key, then fill in the numerator and denominator; ↑ ↓ jump between them. It exports as 2\frac{3}{4}.

3. Roots, powers and subscripts

√ wraps the selection in a square root, or opens an empty one. ⁿ√ puts the caret in the small index first; press → to move into the root. 𝑥ⁿ and 𝑥ₙ open a raised or lowered box, and 𝑥² inserts a square. Press → to leave the box. A power and a subscript on the same symbol are stacked.

4. Trigonometry

sin▾ and sin⁻¹▾ open a menu with sin, cos, tan and their inverses. Choosing one inserts the name followed by a pair of brackets with the caret between them. Inverses show as sin⁻¹ and export as \sin^{-1}.

5. Symbols and brackets

( ) grow automatically to fit tall content such as fractions. ≤ ≥ ≠ ± π θ are on the keypad. Letters you type become italic variables, function names stay upright. × exports as \times and ÷ as \div.

6. Undo and redo

Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes, Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes, or use the curved-arrow keys. Up to 300 changes are remembered; moving the caret is not counted.

7. Plain text to LaTeX

Click “Plain text to LaTeX” on the left to open the box above the keypad. Type an expression and press Enter or → Equation. The result is inserted at the caret (replacing a selection) and nothing else is cleared, so you can keep editing with the keypad. The converter is a fixed set of rules, no guessing:

Order of operations. Powers bind tightest, then implicit multiplication (2x, xy, 2(x+1), 3sin x), then * and /, then + and −. * and · become ×. Letter runs that are not a known name are split into separate variables, so xy is x·y and sinx is sin x.

Denominators. The bottom of a fraction is the implicit-multiplication group right after the /, so 1/2x is 1 over 2x. One special rule: when the top is a bracketed group containing + or −, and the bottom is not bracketed, everything after the / up to the next = , < , > or closing bracket becomes the bottom. That is why (2x^2+14x+24)/x^2-5x-36 gives the whole x²−5x−36 underneath. If you want something different, bracket the bottom yourself: (2x^2+14x+24)/(x^2)-5x-36.

Exponents and indices. ^ takes only the next thing: a number, a letter, a function, or a bracketed group. x^2y is x² times y; use x^(2y) for both. A leading − or + is allowed (e^-x).

Brackets. Brackets directly around a numerator, denominator, exponent or root are removed because the structure already shows them. [ ] and { } are treated like ( ). A missing closing bracket is added. Anything the converter does not recognise (for example % or !) is kept as typed.

8. The LaTeX panel

Open it with the LaTeX button under Copy image. It updates live. Choose Plain, $…$ (inline) or $$…$$ (display) and press Generate & copy. Brackets become \left( … \right) so they size themselves in LaTeX like they do here. Fractions are \frac{…}{…}, roots \sqrt[n]{…}, powers ^{…}, subscripts _{…}.

9. Exporting images

PNG saves a high-resolution transparent image (4× the on-screen size). PNG 8× doubles that again, capped at about 15,000 pixels on the long side. SVG saves a vector file; it uses text, so it relies on the viewer having a maths font and some programs (and Discord, which does not preview SVG) will not show it properly. Copy image puts the PNG on your clipboard to paste into Word or Docs; your browser may block this, in which case use Save PNG. The Black text / White text button on the left, under Plain text to LaTeX, switches the exported text colour. Use black text for light backgrounds. Each export asks you to confirm the save.

10. Saved equations

Open the Saved equations drawer on the left. Give the equation an optional name and press Save current. Load puts a saved equation in the display (replacing the current one), Update overwrites it with what is on screen, TeX copies its LaTeX and ✕ deletes it. Saved equations live in this browser only; clearing site data erases them.

11. Keybinds

Keybinds lists every action in groups, in several columns, with a search box. Click the key box, then press the key you want; clicking the box again cancels. ✕ unbinds, and Reset defaults restores the original keys. If you bind a key that is already used, the old action loses it. Any unbound letter key simply types that variable. Settings are saved in this browser.

12. Customize keypad

Press “Customize keypad” (below the keypad, one scroll down). The left side shows your keypad as rows of slots; the right side is a library of every key: numbers, operators, brackets including [ ] and { }, structures, trigonometry and functions, Greek letters (lower and upper case), calculus keys, and symbols for sets, logic and arrows. Click a slot, then click a library key to put it there. The row buttons move a row up or down, add a key to it (up to 6 per row) or delete it. “Remove selected key” and “+ Add row” change the shape. Everything saves in this browser; “Reset to default” restores the original keypad. Every library key can also get a keyboard shortcut in Keybinds.

Sum, product, integral, limit and unions are structures with a lower and an upper box (limit has only a lower one). The ∑ ∏ ⋃ ⋂ and lim keys place the boxes below and above; the definite integral ∫ₐᵇ places them at the side like in print. Press → to move from the lower to the upper box, or use ↑ ↓. The plain ∫ ∬ ∭ ∮ keys insert just the sign, and the d key inserts an upright differential for things like ∫ f(x) dx. Curly, square and round brackets grow to fit tall content.

13. Light and dark mode

The switch with the sun or moon icon changes the whole page and follows your system until you change it. Scroll down one step to find it, together with Keybinds and this guide. On desktop, the corner button on the display shows only the display, fullscreen, with the equation scaled up to fill it; press Esc or the same button to leave.
